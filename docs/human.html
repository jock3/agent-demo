05 — Facts

Six things
the leaflet left out

Every line below can be looked up. That's the whole difference from what usually gets written about bananas.

Faintly radioactive

The potassium contains the isotope potassium-40. The dose is measurable enough that radiation safety people use it as an informal unit: the banana equivalent dose, roughly 0.1 microsieverts per banana.

0.07 of friction

The 2014 Ig Nobel Prize in physics went to a Japanese team who measured the friction between a shoe and a banana skin on a floor. The slapstick is peer-reviewed.

You're eating a clone

Every export banana is a Cavendish and genetically identical. Its predecessor, Gros Michel, was wiped out by a fungus in the 1950s. The same kind of fungus is on its way again.

It's a berry

Botanically speaking. The plant is a herb rather than a tree, and one of the largest in the world.

It floats

Drop one in water and see for yourself. Its density is lower than water's.

It bends towards the light

The fruit grows upwards towards the sun while the bunch hangs down. That's why it's curved.

MYTHS

Somebody else made this up

"Cures a hangover"  — no. Potassium replaces electrolytes, nothing more.
"Boosts your sex drive"  — no. Entirely invented.
"Nature's own energy boost"  — marketing, not fact.

Health claims on food are regulated. We stick to what can be evidenced, because we want to and because we have to.
